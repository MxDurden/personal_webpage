## The 3 AM Wake-Up Call

Nothing teaches you like a production outage at 3 AM. After two decades, I've learned that most incidents follow patterns.

## Documentation Saves Lives

The worst outages happen when tribal knowledge walks out the door. Write everything down. Your future self (or your replacement) will thank you.

## Automation Is Not Optional

If you're doing it twice, automate it. But test your automation more than you test your code.

## Blameless Post-Mortems

The goal is learning, not punishment. Systems fail. Humans make mistakes. Accept both and improve processes.

## Monitor Everything

You can't fix what you can't see. But also, alert fatigue is real. Be strategic about what wakes people up.

## The Human Factor

Technical problems are usually easy. Communication during an incident is hard. Practice your incident response procedures.

## Embrace Chaos Engineering

Break things intentionally in controlled ways. It's better than having production break things for you.

## Final Thought

Every incident is a gift of knowledge. Collect them. Learn from them. Share them.
